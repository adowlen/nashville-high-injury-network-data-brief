## Join hygiene (Section 10.2)
- The fusion to the 2026-11-02 crash layer is a street-name match, not a proximity join and not a segment join: a crash name-matches when either side of its Street_Address, after house-number stripping and PKE->PIKE / AV->AVE expansion, equals a HIN full_name. Match rates: 222,279 of 222,902 known-count crashes carry an address (99.7 percent match-eligible; 623 missing = 0.3 percent, below the about-5-percent flag threshold, and their severity mix cannot be checked by street because the missing field is the street itself); 172 of 175 HIN street names matched at least one crash. The 3 unmatched names are name-form differences (OLD HICKORY BLVD RAMP, REP JOHN LEWIS WAY N, REV KELLY M SMITH WAY), disclosed in stats.json. Because HIN segments are specific stretches, the name match is an upper bound on whole-street presence and is never printed as on-network crashes.
- Physical scale: network = 297 segments / 226.3 miles (length_mi) / 175 named streets, printed alongside every segment count.
- Built-in internal control group: the name-match run itself is the control - it returns 109,443 name-matched crashes against the HIN layer's own recorded frequency total of 29,530 (3.7x). That gap is the expected signature of whole-street matching vs segment-level accounting; it is recorded here and printed in the brief, and it is why no name-matched figure carries an on-network reading. A second control: the crash layer's injury-or-fatal rate is 32.79 percent on name-matched crashes and 28.72 percent on non-matched crashes (stats.json fusion block, rates on the injury-or-fatal measure, not KSI) - a modest difference, consistent with network streets being busier arterials, and not printed as a severity finding because the measures and windows differ.

## Methods checklist (brief-writing standards, Section 3)
- Base rates and exposure: no traffic-volume or other exposure field exists in the layer. Stated prominently in the brief's second paragraph; per-mile figures use length as the denominator and are labeled; no exposure rate and no causal pattern is claimed.
- Direction of causality: considered and printed in the brief and Methods - tier membership was constructed from crash history (Alta weighted index field), so tier-vs-count comparisons partly describe the selection method; the brief's interpretation section carries that as the strongest alternative explanation.
- Category and definition changes over the window: the layer states no window and carries no dates, so no trend is printed and no definition-change comparison arises. Category is the layer's own field, used as recorded.
- Exact date range: snapshot pulled November 14, 2026; service data last edit 2025-01-03. The counts' study window is not stated in the layer - flagged prominently in the brief, in reviewer notes as an incomplete check, and in the Who can act section as the publishers' fixable gap.
- Match rate for every join: street-name match rates printed above and in stats.json (99.7 percent address-present; 172 of 175 names matched).
- Small counts: every percentage is printed with its underlying count. The smallest group given a percentage is the High tier (16 segments, 372 KSI crashes - the segment count is small, the crash count is not; the brief prints both numbers wherever the tier is characterized). Segment-level per-mile figures are not ranked at all, because short segments (minimum 0.03 miles) make them length artifacts; per-mile rankings use tiers and streets aggregated over at least 1 network mile.
- Official definitions: field aliases from the layer metadata are the definitions used (frequency = "Number of Crashes on this segment"; sum_ksi_cr = "Sum of KSI crashes on this segment"; Category = "Priority Tiers"; hin_59_per = "Road is on the HIN"). KSI is used only in the layer's own crash-count sense, never as the Action Plan's people counts.
